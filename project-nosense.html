The machines

A penny press is a hand-cranked mechanism with a payment system bolted on. The handle drives a spur gear train and a pair of rollers, one of them engraved, and the timing between the selection wheel, the coin drop and the engraving decides whether the print comes out clean and straight. Medallion machines are electronic dispensers of three types of collector coins, paid through the same Nayax system.

The five steps below follow a coin through the machine.

  1. ChooseThe customer turns the handle until their design lines up with the red arrow on the selection wheel.
  2. PayThe Nayax terminal takes card or phone payment and sends a pulse to the hopper.
  3. DispenseThe hopper’s slotted wheel drops a single penny into the coin chute.
  4. TimeA spring-loaded dispensing rod, tripped by ridges on the selection wheel, releases the penny as the engraving comes round.
  5. PressThe handle drives the spur gears and two rollers, one engraved, which flatten and imprint the penny.

Field cases

Three jobs from my files, in date order: new sign letters, a missing pin, and a measured survey.

  1. Three replacement sign letters

    Three letters on the SOUVENIR PRESS sign above a penny press needed replacing. On 5 August 2024 I made card templates from the sign, and that evening drew the N, P and E in SolidWorks as 3 mm plates, 55 mm tall, with a drawing sheet for each. The new letters, still in their protective film, and the sign were photographed on 19 August.

  2. A missing pin

    A penny press had lost the pin in its roller shaft. On 12 August 2024 I photographed and marked up the fault, made a temporary fix in the keyway, and the same day modelled a replacement pin in SolidWorks: 25 mm long, with a 4.6 mm by 4.9 to 5.1 mm section.

  3. A measured site survey

    On 5 December 2024 I measured two penny presses on site and recorded the cabinet and panel dimensions on a measurement sheet, then drew one of them up in SolidWorks as front and side elevations.

The guide

I wrote the Penny Press and Medallion Coin Vending Machine Troubleshooting Guide as a shared reference for the technician team.

Machine overviews
How the penny press and the medallion dispenser work, and what a good result looks like: a clear, straight print, and a coin that lands upright.
Component faults
Sections on the hopper (symptoms, causes and step-by-step fixes), the Nayax payment terminal (connection faults), the rollers and coin chute (misprints, alignment and the dispensing rod), and a gearbox setup video.
Servicing protocol
Tools to carry, safety while working around the public, and what to do before arriving: call the site, complete inductions, bring the right keys and passes.
Technician notes
A shared section for findings from the field and possible improvements.

Possible improvements listed in the guide

  • Cleaning pads on the rollers, so oil spreads evenly and prints stay clean.
  • Supports to lock the coin chute in place, so it cannot move out of alignment.
  • A chamfer filed on the end of the chute, so the coin drops closer to the engraving.

The guide is an internal NoSense document, so it is described here rather than published.
