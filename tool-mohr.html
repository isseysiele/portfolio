Tool · Stress, strain and second moments

Mohr’s circlecalculator

A small tool I wrote while studying mechanics of materials. It began as a plane-stress calculator, and I have since extended it to plane strain, second moments of area, rotation to any angle and yield checks. Pick a mode, enter three values, and it gives the centre and radius of the circle, the principal values and their direction, and draws the circle with the state points. It is a study aid and I have not validated it for design use.

Sign conventions

  • Normal stress is positive in tension. τxy is positive when it acts in the +y direction on the face whose outward normal is +x, which is the usual tensor sign. Equilibrium then fixes the shear on the other faces.
  • The circle has σ across and τ up. Point A is (σx, τxy) and point B is (σy, −τxy), so a positive τxy puts A above the σ axis.
  • Angles are positive counterclockwise, from the x axis towards the y axis. Turning the element counterclockwise by θ moves the point clockwise round the circle by 2θ. A book that plots positive shear downwards shows the circle turning the other way.
  • θp is counterclockwise from the x axis to the plane that carries σ1, and the plane for σ2 is 90° further round. The maximum shear button turns to the angle where τx′y′ = +R, which is the top of the circle, at θp − 45°.

Method

All three modes use the same circle. I describe it for stress first, then say what changes for strain and for second moments of area.

  • Centre: C = (σx + σy) / 2.
  • Radius: R = √(h² + τxy²), where h = (σx − σy) / 2.
  • Principal stresses: σ1 = C + R and σ2 = C − R, where the circle crosses the σ axis.
  • Maximum in-plane shear: τmax = R, at the top and bottom of the circle.
  • Principal angle: tan 2θp = 2τxy / (σx − σy). The angle θp runs counterclockwise from the x axis to the plane that carries σ1, and the plane that carries σ2 is 90° further round. On the circle it is half the angle from point A round to the σ1 intercept, measured clockwise.
  • Rotation: for an element turned counterclockwise by θ, σx′ = C + h cos 2θ + τxy sin 2θ, σy′ = C − h cos 2θ − τxy sin 2θ, and τx′y′ = −h sin 2θ + τxy cos 2θ. The sum σx′ + σy′ does not change with θ, and τx′y′ is zero at θp.

Point A is (σx, τxy) and point B is (σy, −τxy), with τ drawn upwards. They sit at opposite ends of a diameter through C, and the turned element is the point at 2θ round from A. If the circle shrinks to a point, because σx = σy and τxy = 0, every direction is principal and the page reports no angle.

Plane strain. Replace σ with ε and τ with γ/2, where γxy is the engineering shear strain. The vertical axis is γ/2, so the circle has the same form. R is half the maximum in-plane shear strain, so γmax = 2R, and the principal angle is ½ atan2(γxy, εx − εy).

Second moments of area. With Ix = ∫y² dA, Iy = ∫x² dA and Ixy = ∫xy dA, the centre is C = (Ix + Iy) / 2 and the radius is R = √(h² + Ixy²) with h = (Ix − Iy) / 2. The principal second moments are I1 = C + R and I2 = C − R, and tan 2θp = −2Ixy / (Ix − Iy). The minus sign comes from the way Ixy is defined. Turning the axes by θ gives Ix′ = C + h cos 2θ − Ixy sin 2θ and Ix′y′ = h sin 2θ + Ixy cos 2θ. The sum Ix + Iy, the polar second moment, does not change.

Yield checks, plane stress only. This is the plane stress case with the out-of-plane stress taken as zero, so the three principal stresses are σ1, σ2 and σ3 = 0. The absolute maximum shear is τabs = (σmax − σmin) / 2 over all three, and the two extra circles, between σ1 and σ3 and between σ2 and σ3, are drawn more lightly. The von Mises equivalent stress is √(σx² − σxσy + σy² + 3τxy²), and the Tresca equivalent stress is σmax − σmin. The factor of safety is the yield strength divided by each of them, and a value below 1 means that criterion predicts yielding.

I first wrote this as a small React page for my own study, for plane stress only. This version is plain JavaScript, with no libraries and no network requests, so it runs on this static site. It also reports the principal angle, draws the τ axis at σ = 0, and uses one scale on both axes so the circle is round and the state points sit on it. The strain and second-moment modes, the rotation, the presets, the shareable link and the yield checks are later additions.

Limits

  • It is a study aid. It has been checked against worked calculations, not against other software or a design code, and it is not validated for design use.
  • The yield checks are for plane stress only, with the normal stress in the z direction and the shears out of the plane taken as zero, so the third principal stress is zero. If a real part has stress out of the plane, the absolute maximum shear, the von Mises stress and the Tresca stress shown here do not apply. The factor of safety is only the yield strength divided by the equivalent stress for one steady load. It allows for no fatigue, stress concentration, buckling or code safety factor.
  • The maximum in-plane shear is the radius of the circle. In plane stress the absolute maximum shear is shown as well, because it is larger when σ1 and σ2 have the same sign.
  • The strain mode turns the strain components you enter. It does not convert strain to stress, and it uses no material properties. The strain presets are the stress presets worked out for E = 200 GPa and ν = 0.3, as examples only.
  • The second-moment mode takes Ix, Iy and Ixy as given. It does not work them out from a shape. Values that cannot come from a real area are flagged but still calculated.
  • It does not convert units. The units label only changes the labels, so every value must be entered in one consistent unit.
  • Results show two decimal places, or more when the values are small. Values larger than 10100 in size are refused.
